Frequently Asked Questions

Which rain sound is best for sleep? expand_more

Rain on a window paired with thunder reliably tops the list because the soft tapping on glass hands the brain a predictable rhythm while distant thunder adds low-frequency calm. Heavy rain on a tin roof and rain in the forest are close runners-up.

Do heavy rain and thunder really help you sleep? expand_more

Yes. A growing body of research on pink noise and broadband ambient sound points to faster sleep onset, deeper slow-wave sleep, and fewer night-time awakenings when ambient rain plays at a comfortable volume.

Is rain sound useful for studying? expand_more

Absolutely. Rain on a window with distant thunder is one of the most popular focus and concentration soundscapes — it covers distractions, lowers stress hormones, and has no lyrics to pull cognitive attention away from your work.

How long should I play rain sounds for sleep? expand_more

Most people do best playing rain sounds for the whole night — six to eight hours, looped if needed. Some prefer a one- or two-hour sleep timer so the audio quietly fades after they're asleep. Both approaches work — experiment and see what feels right.

Can I use rain sounds for meditation and yoga? expand_more

Yes. Heavy rain and rolling thunder pair beautifully with mindfulness, breathwork, yin yoga, and guided meditation. Keep the volume low enough that your guide's voice (or your own breath) is still clearly in front.

Is this recording real or AI-generated? expand_more

Real. The audio was captured on location at a mountain hotel with a Zoom H2N microphone and finished in Adobe Audition by Relaxing Ambience ASMR — no synthetic loops or AI effects.
